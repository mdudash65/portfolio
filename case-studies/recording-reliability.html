SANITIZED ENGINEERING CASE STUDY · ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT

Recording reliability under resource contention

Role: System architecture, technical direction, engineering oversight, evidence-based acceptance
Context: ShowPilot Flight Recorder, a local-first recording and analysis application under active field testing

Executive summary

During development and field testing, concurrent live recording and local transcription exposed significant CPU and thermal pressure, creating a potential threat to recording reliability.

The assessment identified a key separation of responsibilities: recording must preserve evidence as it occurs, while transcription and AI-assisted analysis can be deferred. The resulting design incorporates durable local capture, persistent processing queues, resource-aware workload management, and explicit recovery boundaries.

Implementation and controlled validation have made progress toward those objectives. Extended thermal and interruption/recovery testing remain necessary before broader reliability claims are justified.

1. Engineering problem

The application captures live-session evidence streams while supporting local transcription and later analysis. Concurrent recording and transcription placed competing demands on CPU and thermal capacity. The question was whether transcription needed to run during capture or could be deferred without sacrificing product requirements.

2. Assessment approach

The investigation examined responsibilities, resource contention, durability, and recovery behavior. It distinguished time-critical operations from deferrable computation, traced capture and processing boundaries, and defined observable acceptance criteria beyond automated tests alone.

3. Architectural finding

Capture and transcription had different service requirements. Capture must durably preserve evidence. Transcription can run later if source audio and work items persist across interruptions. Treating both as immediately necessary increased contention without a corresponding product benefit.

4. Architectural response

  • Incremental local recording with durable persistence.
  • Audio staging independent of immediate transcription.
  • A persistent queue for deferred transcription.
  • Resource-aware admission control for expensive processing.
  • Explicit export and recovery boundaries.
  • Operational evidence to assess recording integrity and recovery outcomes.

5. Fault tolerance and recoverability

Fault tolerance means preserving essential capability despite certain failures. Recoverability means restoring a valid state after interruption. The architecture seeks to let capture proceed when enrichment work fails or becomes unavailable and to protect previously committed evidence if processing stops.

An abrupt browser or computer shutdown remains a critical acceptance scenario. Durable commits can limit the loss window, but no local application can preserve bytes never committed to durable storage. Abrupt-shutdown and restart behavior requires explicit validation; this is not presented as a completed guarantee.

6. Operational impact and validation

The design aims to reduce capture disruption from transcription activity, improve interruption resilience, and isolate future transcription-engine changes. Portions of the architecture have been implemented and exercised through automated tests and controlled checks. Extended multi-session thermal evaluation and interruption/recovery acceptance remain open.

7. Engineering principle

Problem → Evidence → Operational impact → Architectural decision → Implementation → Validation.

The objective is understandable system boundaries, evidence preservation, and recommendations whose assumptions and effectiveness can be tested.

This document is a sanitized account of engineering work on an application under active development. It is not a completed third-party production audit or a certification of production reliability. It excludes private code, captured content, credentials, and internal operational records.
