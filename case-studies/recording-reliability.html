Recording reliability under resource contention

How a local-first recording system separated critical evidence capture from expensive transcription, and why recovery and observability were treated as architectural requirements.

The engineering problem

Concurrent recording and local speech transcription created CPU and thermal pressure. The consequential risk was not simply slower analysis: expensive downstream work could compete with time-sensitive evidence capture. A reliable system needed to protect recording first and tolerate deferred interpretation.

Architecture decision

Live session
events + audio→Durable capture
journal + staging→Deferred jobs
local queue→Transcription
when admitted

The architecture establishes a priority boundary: preserve observed evidence before running expensive inference. A service-worker-owned IndexedDB append journal protects structured recording data; audio staging and a local durable job queue allow transcription to occur outside the capture-critical path. An admission governor can withhold expensive processing during active recording.

Engineering evidence snapshot

ObservedHigh CPU/thermal pressure during concurrent capture and local transcription, especially with multiple rooms.
DecisionKeep capture on the critical path; make transcription deferrable and independently recoverable.
ImplementedIncremental capture summaries, durable append journal, audio staging, local queued transcription, and inference admission controls.
Still to validateExtended multi-session thermal behavior and interruption/recovery acceptance under representative operating conditions.

How the assessment was conducted

The work combined architecture-level mapping, controlled observation, operational instrumentation, test evidence, and review of consequential implementation boundaries. It emphasized state ownership, durable persistence, export reconstruction, resource contention, and the distinction between passing automated tests and demonstrated runtime reliability.

Impact and limits

The revised design provides a clearer separation of responsibilities and a basis for protecting capture continuity while analysis is postponed. This is a design and implementation outcome, not a claim of completed production reliability certification. Extended thermal and interruption/recovery testing remains open.

Transferable principle

Protect irreversible work first. When a workflow combines time-critical acquisition with expensive interpretation, make acquisition durable and independently recoverable before optimizing the interpretation stage.
